02Studio

Most new worlds never get built.

Even a mobile game costs thousands of dollars1 before anyone plays it, and what it earns back often doesn’t cover that2. So the odd, small worlds stay on paper. We want to close that gap: build our own tools, cut what a game costs to make, and spend the difference on worlds players want to explore. Our first one is nearly ready. Made by people who play, for everyone who does: the hobbyist, the marketing CEO, the lady behind the counter.

  1. Kevuru Games, Mobile Game Development Cost, 2026: a simple 2D game from $10,000 to $20,000, a casual game from $30,000.
  2. SuperScale, Good Games Don’t Die, 2023: of 504 UK and US developers surveyed, 43% of games were cancelled before launch and 83% of launched games failed within three years.

03Platform

Built for one game. Kept for the next.

Our first game started as a side project. The pipeline we built to ship it is becoming the TagoMind Gaming platform: AI writes the code, runs the builds and tests, and checks the balance maths, so the next game costs less than the last. The art, the story and every call stay with people.
